What is in the plate

Far-UV (about 135–178 nm) and near-UV (about 177–283 nm) come from the public GALEX GR6/7 HiPS at CDS, resampled to this oval. Where both bands were imaged, the colour is their ratio. Where only near-UV was imaged, far-UV is scaled from the near-UV of similar dust. Where GALEX has no coverage, the pixel is the median ultraviolet of observed sky at a similar 12 µm brightness, except the bright inner plane, which is left faint because thick dust absorbs ultraviolet light. Turn estimates off to see only the GALEX pixels.

Visible light is the Gaia DR3 red, green, and blue flux map. Infrared is the WISE 12 µm WSSA dust map. Earth uses an Aura/OMI ozone composite for January 2016. In that composite the polar-night gap reaches about 66° N, and the cap is copied from the first bright latitude. Night lights are the VIIRS Black Marble, and the infrared Earth is a July 2016 MODIS land-surface temperature composite.

This is a practical plate, a few arcminutes per pixel. It is not the unpublished nside 2048 map, and it does not include Swift-UVOT, FIMS/SPEAR, TD-1, or the catalogue of individual Gaia stars.

Data: GALEX GR6/7 (NASA/STScI, HiPS by CDS), Gaia DR3 (ESA/Gaia/DPAC), WSSA 12 µm (Meisner & Finkbeiner 2014; NASA/JPL-Caltech), Aura/OMI ozone, VIIRS Black Marble, and MODIS land-surface temperature (NASA).
